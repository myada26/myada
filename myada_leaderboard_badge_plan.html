Leaderboard + Badge Implementation Plan

MyADA — a shared reference for the whole team. No code, just logic and flow.

What this plan covers
Purpose
This document defines how the Leaderboard and Badge features in MyADA should work — from a student's perspective, from an instructor's perspective, and from a logic perspective. It covers what triggers a point, what triggers a badge, how the leaderboard rank is decided, and what the student sees on screen. It does not describe code.
Two features, one goal
Both the Leaderboard and Badges exist for the same reason: to recognize effort and keep students motivated, especially in a low-connectivity environment where they are often learning alone. A student who earns a badge should feel that their hard work was noticed. A student who climbs the leaderboard should feel that consistent practice pays off — not just raw talent.
The two screens at a glance
Leaderboard (Ranks tab)
Shows a ranked list of all students in the class by their total accumulated points. The student can see their own rank pinned at the top, and scroll through the rest of the class below.
Badges tab
Shows all badges — earned and locked. Earned badges display when the student got them. Locked badges show a progress bar so the student can see how close they are.
Key design principles
Effort, not just talent. A student who logs in every day and finishes modules step by step should rank higher than someone who aces one quiz and disappears. The point system is designed to reward consistency.
Offline-first. A student earns points and badges even without internet. The app tracks everything locally and updates the leaderboard the next time the device connects.
Prototype scope. For the capstone evaluation, a small group of testers with seeded data is enough to demonstrate the leaderboard and badges working. Full live multi-user syncing is a future version concern.
What points are and why they matter
Points are the currency of the leaderboard. Every meaningful action a student takes in MyADA adds points to their total score. The total score decides their rank. Points never go away — they accumulate forever. The leaderboard always shows the all-time total.
Group 1 — Daily login and consistency
Daily login
Every day a student opens the app counts as an active day. The app checks whether they opened it yesterday. If they did, the streak grows. If they missed a day, the streak resets to 1.
Opens app on any given day+2 points
Maintains a 7-day streak+15 points (one-time bonus)
Maintains a 30-day streak+50 points (one-time bonus)
Misses a day — streak resetsNo penalty, streak restarts at 1
Not yet implemented. On every app open, compare today's date to the last recorded active date. If it is a new day, award 2 points and update the active date. If it is the same day, do nothing.
Group 2 — Quiz completion
Taking and finishing a quiz
Points from quizzes come in two parts: a base reward for finishing, plus bonus points depending on how well the student did.
Submits a quiz (score 50% or above)+10 points
Scores between 70% and 89%+5 points bonus
Scores between 90% and 99%+10 points bonus
Scores 100% (perfect)+15 points bonus
Retakes a quiz and improves their score+3 points
Retakes a quiz but does not improve+0 points
Scores below 50% — quiz not credited+0 points, retake encouraged
Group 3 — Coding exercises (IDE)
Completing exercises in the code editor
Every exercise completed earns a base reward. Extra points come from how the student completed it — whether they needed hints, how fast they finished, and whether they got it right the first time.
Completes any coding exercise+5 points
First-try correct — no hints, first submission passes+5 points bonus
Completes a timed challenge within the time limit+8 points
Uses a hint during an exercise−1 point per hint used
Skips an exercise−1 point
Not yet implemented. The exercise screen needs to track: (1) how many hints were used per attempt, (2) whether the first submission was correct, and (3) for timed challenges, whether it was completed within the time window. These are passed to the scoring logic at the moment the student submits.
Group 4 — Module and milestone completion
Finishing a full module or major milestone
A module is complete when the student has finished all lessons inside it and submitted the final quiz.
Completes a full module (all lessons + quiz done)+20 points
Earns any badge+10 points
Earns a certificate for a completed module+30 points
Completes the diagnostic assessment (one-time only)+5 points
How total score is calculated
A student's total score is the sum of all points they have ever earned across all groups. There is no decay, no expiry, and no reset. Every action is added on top of what came before. When the leaderboard is shown, students are ranked by this total, highest to lowest.
What the leaderboard screen shows
The leaderboard lives in the same screen as Badges and Certificates — it is the first tab. It ranks all students in the class by their total points. The goal of the screen is to give each student a sense of where they stand and motivate them to keep going.
The five parts of the ranks tab
1
Filter chips — All time / This week
Two buttons at the top. "All time" shows the student's full accumulated score. "This week" shows only points earned in the last 7 days. This lets students who started late still feel competitive within a shorter window.
2
Your card — pinned at the top
The logged-in student's own rank is always pinned at the top of the screen so they never have to scroll to find themselves. It shows their name, avatar initials, current rank number, total score, and how many points separate them from the person directly above them — for example, "48 points behind rank 3."
3
Top 3 podium
The top three students are shown in a visual podium format: rank 1 in the center and slightly elevated, rank 2 on the left, rank 3 on the right. Each shows the student's name, avatar initials, and total score.
4
Scrollable list — rank 4 and below
Each row shows: rank number, avatar initials, student name, total score, and a small indicator showing whether their rank went up, went down, or stayed the same since the last sync. This helps students see that ranks are actively changing.
5
Last updated notice
A small note at the bottom shows when the leaderboard data was last synced. If the student is offline, this reminds them that rankings reflect the last available data. Points earned while offline are saved locally and reflected after the next sync.
How ranking works offline
What happens when there is no internet
Points are still earned and saved on the student's device. However, the student's position among classmates cannot update without knowing everyone else's current scores. While offline, the student sees their last known rank and their own running point total. When the app reconnects, locally saved points are sent to the server, ranks are recalculated, and the leaderboard updates on next open.
What badges are and how they work
Badges are permanent awards given when a student meets a specific condition. Once earned, a badge is never taken away. Each badge checks one rule: either a count (do something X times), a quality (do something at a certain standard), or a milestone (reach a specific point in the learning path). The badge check runs automatically every time a relevant action is completed.
Category 1 — Getting started
◈
First step
Beginner
Awarded when: the student completes the diagnostic assessment for the first time.
Every student starts somewhere. This badge acknowledges the courage it takes to begin, and ensures every new user earns something on their very first day.
◈
Path found
Beginner
Awarded when: the student's personalized learning path is generated (immediately after the diagnostic).
Reinforces that the app has understood the student and created something just for them.
Category 2 — Consistency and streaks
▲
Showing up
Streak
Awarded when: the student logs in on 3 consecutive days.
The hardest habit to build is simply returning. Three days is the first real signal that a student is forming a routine.
▲
Week warrior
Streak
Awarded when: the student logs in on 7 consecutive days.
A full week of engagement is a strong predictor of course completion.
▲
Unstoppable
StreakRare
Awarded when: the student logs in on 30 consecutive days.
The rarest streak badge. Very few students will earn it, which makes those who do stand out on the leaderboard.
Category 3 — Quiz performance
◆
Quiz taker
Quiz
Awarded when: the student submits their very first quiz.
Quizzes can feel intimidating. This badge lowers the barrier by rewarding the act of trying, regardless of the result.
◆
Perfect score
QuizRare
Awarded when: the student scores 100% on any quiz for the first time.
Marks genuine mastery of a topic. A student who earns this has demonstrated full understanding — not just passing.
◆
Quiz streak
Quiz
Awarded when: the student scores 70% or above on 5 quizzes in a row (not necessarily on the same day).
Rewards consistent quality, not just one-off good performances.
◆
Comeback
Quiz
Awarded when: the student retakes a quiz they previously failed (below 50%) and passes it this time (50% or above).
Specifically encourages resilience. Failing and retrying is exactly the behavior we want to reward.
Category 4 — Coding exercises
●
First code
Coding
Awarded when: the student completes their very first coding exercise in the IDE.
Writing and running actual code for the first time is a significant moment. This badge marks it.
●
Clean shot
Coding
Awarded when: the student completes a coding exercise correctly on the very first submission, with no hints used.
Rewards confidence and preparation. A student who gets it right on the first try without help has truly understood the concept.
●
Bug hunter
Coding
Awarded when: the student completes 5 debugging exercises (exercises tagged as debug-type) in total.
Debugging is one of the most important and underrated programming skills. Calling it out with its own badge signals its importance.
●
Speed coder
CodingRare
Awarded when: the student completes a timed challenge and submits a correct answer before the timer runs out.
Working under time pressure is a real skill. This badge rewards students who have become fast and confident enough to perform on demand.
Category 5 — Module and learning path progress
■
Module 1 complete
Progress
Awarded when: the student finishes all lessons and the quiz inside Module 1. One badge exists per module, awarded in sequence.
Marks a completed chapter. These are the most predictable badges — every student who progresses will earn them, ensuring even slower learners feel rewarded.
■
Halfway there
Progress
Awarded when: the student completes 50% of their total assigned learning path (by module count).
The middle of a learning journey is where most students drop off. This badge creates a visible milestone to push through the slump.
■
Path completed
ProgressRare
Awarded when: the student completes all modules in their assigned learning path.
The highest achievement badge. A student who earns this has finished the entire course.
How the badge check works (plain logic)
The badge check runs after every action
Whenever a student completes an exercise, finishes a quiz, logs in, or reaches a milestone, the app checks whether any badge condition has now been met. If it has — and the student does not already have that badge — the badge is awarded immediately. The student sees a badge reveal on the results screen. Ten points are added to their total score automatically. The badge is marked as earned with today's date.
Locked badges show progress, not just a lock
For badges that require doing something multiple times (e.g., 5 debugging exercises), the app tracks the student's current count and displays it as a progress bar. For example: "Bug hunter — 3 of 5 debugging exercises done." This turns a locked badge from a wall into a goal.
Note for the team: The badges for "Clean shot" (first-try correct) and "Speed coder" (timed challenge) require tracking that does not yet exist in the exercise screen. When those features are built, they need to pass the result to the badge check after submission.
The guiding principle
The leaderboard and badge screens are display layers. They can only show data that already exists. This means the scoring and tracking logic must be in place before the screens are built. Build the engine first, then the dashboard.
Recommended build order
1
Quiz submission scoring start here
When a student submits a quiz, the result (score percentage) must trigger a point calculation. The base points plus any accuracy bonus are recorded. This is the most foundational piece — quizzes are already in the app, they just need a scoring hook at the moment of submission.
2
Module completion detection
The app needs to know when a student has finished everything inside a module — all lessons read and the quiz submitted. When that condition is met, 20 points are awarded, a module badge is checked, and a certificate is generated.
3
Daily login and streak tracking
On every app open, the app compares today's date to the last recorded active date. If it is a new day: add 2 points, increment the streak counter, update the last active date. If the streak reaches 7 or 30, fire the streak bonus and check the streak badges. This logic belongs in the app startup flow.
4
Exercise tracking additions requires new screen logic
The exercise screen needs three new tracking behaviors: (1) count how many hints the student used, (2) record whether the first submission was correct, and (3) for timed challenges, record whether submission happened before the timer hit zero. These values are passed to scoring only at the moment the student submits.
5
Badge check engine
After every scoring event, the badge engine checks whether any badge condition has now been satisfied for this student. If yes, the badge is awarded, 10 points are added, and the badge reveal is queued. This runs silently in the background — the student only sees the result, not the check.
6
Badge screen and progress bars UI layer
Once the badge engine is running, the Badges tab can be built. It reads the list of all badges, checks which ones the student has earned, and for locked badges with partial progress (like "Bug hunter"), displays a progress bar. The student can tap any badge to see its rule, category, and earned date.
7
Leaderboard screen build last
The Ranks tab is the final piece. By this point, every student has a total score built from quizzes, exercises, logins, and milestones. The leaderboard sorts students by that number, renders the podium for the top 3, shows the scrollable list below, and pins the current student's card at the top.
For the prototype evaluation
What is enough for the SUS usability test
The usability test does not need live multi-user data. Seed the evaluation group with a small set of test accounts that have different point totals — enough to populate the podium and a short list below. The badge system should work live: evaluators should be able to complete a quiz or exercise during the test and see a badge trigger in real time.
